# 2026-2027年的日中關係展望 (Japan-China Relations Outlook 2026-2027)

研究日期：2026-10-30｜資料時間範圍：2025-10 起（高市政權成立至今）｜項目數：14

## 摘要

**核心判斷：2026 年底到 2027 年，日中關係最可能維持「受控的冷和平」——政治凍結、灰色地帶摩擦常態化，但雙方都避免軍事衝突，貨物貿易也大致維持。真正的破冰窗口要等 2027 年秋季中共二十一大之後，而且前提是雙方找到一套「保全面子」的台灣表述方式。**

1. **僵局的根源還沒解開。** 2025 年 11 月 7 日，高市早苗首相在國會表示「台灣有事」可能構成日本的「存立危機事態」。中方此後一直要求她正式撤回這段發言。自那時起，兩國沒有舉行過首腦會談或外長會談。2026 年 9 月 28 日，前外相岩屋毅向王毅提議，由兩國政府重申 1972 年《日中聯合聲明》的精神，作為「事實上的撤回」；王毅拒絕了「曖昧做法」，但提到 2027 年是邦交正常化 55 週年。
2. **中方對日施壓已經制度化，涵蓋經濟、人員往來和軍事三方面：**
   - 經濟：再度停止進口日本水產品；2026 年 1 月 6 日禁止對日本軍事用戶出口兩用物項；2 月和 6 月兩度把日本企業列入出口管制名單。2026 年上半年，受管制稀土對日出口年減 51%（日經分析中國海關資料）；據報導，鏑、鋱對日出口在上半年為零，但有資料只顯示其中 4 個月為零，這一點尚未完全證實。
   - 人員往來：發布赴日旅遊勸阻；2026 年 1–9 月中國大陸訪日旅客約 333 萬人次，年減 55.5%；1 月 27 日日本最後兩隻熊貓返華。
   - 軍事：2025 年 12 月 6 日殲-15 對日本 F-15 進行火控雷達照射；中國海警 2025 年在釣魚台鄰接區出現 356 天，創下紀錄（日本海上保安廳統計；中方自稱 357 天）；中俄 2025 年 12 月、2026 年 6 月兩度聯合轟炸機巡航，7 月又進行聯合海上巡航，航線近乎環繞日本。
3. **經濟面「政冷經溫」，但正在轉冷：**
   - 貨物貿易仍然成長：2026 年上半年日本對華出口增 14.3%，自華進口增 15.7%。
   - 投資和人員往來明顯降溫：在華日企降至 10,118 家，是帝國資料庫 2010 年開始調查以來最低。
4. **日本國內政治提高了雙方讓步的成本：**
   - 2026 年 2 月 8 日眾院選舉，自民黨單獨取得 316 席，高市不太可能撤回發言。
   - 2026 年 12 月要定稿的安保三文件，以及非核三原則中「不運進」原則是否修改，是下一個升級觸發點。
   - 高市內閣支持率在 10 月降到任內最低（時事通信社民調 42.4%）。
5. **美中緩和讓日本有「被越頂」的風險。** 川普 2026 年 5 月訪問北京，習近平 9 月回訪華府，美中關係趨穩，北京因此更能承受繼續孤立東京的代價。據報導川普曾勸習近平與高市見面，習未接受。這個說法來自 SCMP 的匿名消息來源，官方未證實；報導對川普提出的時間也說法不一：日本時報稱是 9 月峰會，時事通信社稱是 5 月。
6. **2027 年是敏感日期最密集的一年：** 7 月 7 日盧溝橋事變 90 週年、8 月 1 日解放軍建軍百年、9 月自民黨總裁選舉、9 月 29 日邦交正常化 55 週年、秋季中共二十一大、12 月 13 日南京大屠殺 90 週年。55 週年可能成為改善關係的契機，其他日期則多半帶來升溫壓力。

The likely ceiling for improvement is a resumed FM-level channel, perhaps on APEC margins or later at a December meeting, plus incremental easing of practical measures (rare-earth licensing for civilian use, airline schedules). Risks are skewed to the downside if a security incident or a new Taiwan-related statement occurs around the 7 Nov anniversary or APEC.

### Taiwan Issue and Security

**current_status**（截至2026年10月的現狀概述）

> The Taiwan question is the core cause of the Japan-China crisis that has run since November 2025. On 7 Nov 2025 PM Takaichi told the Diet that a Chinese use of force against Taiwan involving warships, including a blockade, 'could constitute a survival-threatening situation', the legal trigger under the 2015 security legislation for collective self-defense. It has internationalized the issue at the UN: Fu Cong's letters of 22 Nov and 1 Dec 2025 called Japanese intervention 'aggression', and Beijing has invoked the Cairo and Potsdam declarations. It is strengthening Nansei island defenses, including missile deployment on Yonaguni about 110 km from Taiwan, and relaxed its defense export rules (Apr 2026). Wang Yi told former FM Iwaya (28 Sep 2026) that 'ambiguous approaches' such as reaffirming the 1972 Communique are not enough. On the '2027 window', the Pentagon's late-2025 report reiterated that the PLA aims for the capability to win a Taiwan fight by 2027, while the US ODNI (Mar 2026) assessed that China is not planning an invasion in 2027 and has no fixed timeline. The ODNI also called Takaichi's remark 'a significant shift' and predicted intensified Chinese coercion against Japan through 2026.

It refuses to retract, avoids further specifics, and is strengthening deterrence (three security documents, Nansei defense, Yonaguni missiles, counter-strike capability). **current_status**（截至2026年10月的現狀概述）

> As of late October 2026 the East China Sea remains the most militarized front of the Japan-China crisis that began with PM Sanae Takaichi's 7 Nov 2025 Diet remark that a Taiwan contingency could be a 'survival-threatening situation'. ### China Russia Joint Military Pressure

*中俄(朝)對日軍事協同：2026年6月中俄聯合轟炸機繞日巡航、聯合海上演習、朝鮮因素，對日本安保認知與日中關係的影響*

#### 基本資訊

**item_name**（研究項目名稱）

China-Russia (and North Korea) joint military pressure on Japan

**current_status**（截至2026年10月的現狀概述）

> China-Russia military coordination around Japan has become routine and more Japan-focused since the Nov 2025 Japan-China crisis. Two joint strategic bomber patrols have flown near Japan in the period: the 10th on 9 Dec 2025 (2 H-6, 2 Tu-95, 4 J-16 escorts; Miyako Strait to the Pacific off Shikoku, a first) - three days after the J-15 radar-lock incident - and the 11th on 27 Jun 2026, flown in two waves (morning: 9 aircraft over the Sea of Japan and back to the ECS; afternoon: 10 aircraft via the Miyako gap to the Pacific off Shikoku, incl. H-6, Tu-95, Tu-142 and J-16), prompting JASDF and ROKAF scrambles and South Korean protests. At sea, Joint Sea/Maritime Interaction-2026 was held off Qingdao on 6-13 Jul 2026, followed by a joint naval patrol (incl. PLAN cruiser Anshan, destroyer Kaifeng, a Russian corvette and oiler) that passed through the Miyako Strait (16 Jul), operated inside Japan's claimed EEZ around Okinotori with live machine-gun fire (19 Jul) and exited via La Perouse to Vladivostok (29 Jul), effectively circling Japan. Both Beijing and Moscow describe Japan's defense build-up as 'new militarism'. Japan's 2026 Defense White Paper (Aug 2026) describes joint China-Russia bomber and naval operations as 'obviously intended as demonstration activities against Japan' and of 'grave concern', and North Korea as a 'more grave and imminent threat than ever', with Russia-DPRK military cooperation strengthening Pyongyang. The effect on Japan-China relations is to harden Japanese threat perception, justify the ongoing revision of the three security documents and Southwest/Northern dual-front posture, and reduce Tokyo's incentive to make concessions on the Taiwan remark. There is no evidence of formal trilateral China-Russia-DPRK exercises.

#### 分析

**key_actors_positions**（主要行為者（日本政府、中國政府、其他）立場）

> Japan: views China-Russia joint flights and sailings as deliberate demonstrations against Japan; protests through diplomatic channels, scrambles, and strengthens US-Japan and Japan-ROK coordination; revising security documents to address a multi-front (Southwest + North) threat. China: describes joint patrols as routine annual cooperation 'not targeting any third party', while officially accusing Japan of 'new militarism' and Taiwan interference. Russia: frames patrols as planned strategic cooperation; hostile to Japan over Ukraine sanctions, has frozen peace treaty talks and echoes Chinese criticism of Japanese rearmament. North Korea: deepening military alliance with Russia (2024 treaty, troops and munitions for Ukraine war) and warming ties with China; continued missile tests. South Korea: protests joint ADIZ entries; cautious about being drawn into Japan-China dispute. United States: responds with bomber flights and large exercises with Japan.

**drivers**（推動關係走向的結構性與短期驅動因素）

> Structural: China-Russia 'no-limits' strategic partnership deepened by Russia's Ukraine war dependence on China; shared opposition to US alliances and Japan's counterstrike build-up; Russia-DPRK alliance and DPRK munitions exports; Japan's alignment with Western sanctions on Russia. Short-term: the Nov 2025 Japan-China crisis gave Beijing incentive to show Tokyo it faces a coordinated front; timing of joint patrols around Japan-China incidents (Dec 2025) and US-Japan exercises; annual exercise calendar (Joint Sea in summer, bomber patrols mid-year and year-end).

**risk_factors**（主要風險因素）

> Accidents during intercepts of mixed formations near Japanese airspace; possible future airspace violation (Russia has violated Japanese airspace before, e.g., Rebun Island 2024); joint naval live-fire inside Japan's EEZ; coordinated pressure during a Taiwan crisis forcing Japan to split forces between north and southwest; Russian technology transfer to China (submarines, early warning) or to DPRK (missile/satellite technology) eroding Japan's deterrence; possible inclusion of DPRK in exercises.

**policy_tools_and_reversibility**（雙方使用的政策工具/措施、啟動日、可逆性與解除條件）

> China/Russia: joint strategic bomber patrols (since Jul 2019; roughly 1-2 per year) - reversible in principle but institutionalized; Joint Sea exercises and joint Pacific naval patrols (annual since 2021) - institutionalized; rhetorical 'anti-militarism' campaigns - easily adjustable. **third_party_variables**（美國、台灣、韓國、俄羅斯、朝鮮、東協、G7等第三方變數的影響方向）

> Russia: core co-actor; a Ukraine ceasefire could free Russian Pacific Fleet assets and increase activity near Japan, or reduce Moscow's dependence on Beijing (mixed). North Korea: missile launches and Russia-DPRK tech transfer raise threat perception; DPRK-China rapprochement after Sept 2025 adds a third axis (escalatory). United States: bomber escorts and exercises (Keen Sword 27) reassure Japan; Trump-Putin and Trump-Xi diplomacy could dilute pressure on Moscow/Beijing (uncertain). **narrative_and_public_opinion**（雙方官方敘事與民意）

#### 展望

**outlook_2027**（2027年展望）

#### 基本資訊

**item_name**（研究項目名稱）

Trade, Investment and Economic Divergence (Japan-China, 2026)

**current_status**（截至2026年10月的現狀概述）

> As of October 2026 Japan-China economic ties show a sharp divergence between trade flows and corporate commitment. Trade in goods measured in yen grew strongly in H1 2026: Japan's Ministry of Finance preliminary data show exports to China up 14.3% y/y to JPY 10.22 trillion and imports from China up 15.7% to JPY 14.87 trillion, widening Japan's bilateral deficit 18.9% to JPY 4.64 trillion. Exports to China kept growing in the fiscal half April-September 2026 (about +17.6% y/y per the MOF fiscal-half release) and September exports to China rose about 8.6% y/y. At the same time, the long-run investment footprint is shrinking: Teikoku Databank's survey (published 28 Sept 2026) counted 10,118 Japanese companies operating in mainland China as of June 2026, the lowest since the survey began, down 22.4% from 13,034 in 2024 and about 30% below the 2012 peak of 14,394, with a record 4,137 withdrawals/untraceable firms versus 1,221 new entries over 2024-2026. Sentiment surveys by the Japanese Chamber of Commerce and Industry in China (CJCCI) show 44% of members expecting their business conditions to worsen in H1 2026 (+7 points), with geopolitical risk the top concern; around half of firms in an NHK survey described doing business with China as difficult. Services-side ties have collapsed: mainland Chinese visitor arrivals to Japan were down 55.5% y/y in January-September 2026 after Beijing's November 2025 travel advisory, and Chinese visitor spending fell about 40% in Q3. Overall the relationship is best described as 'cold politics, lukewarm/selectively hot trade, cold investment' - headline trade values remain resilient because of supply-chain interdependence (Chinese intermediate goods, Japanese capital goods and components) and yen-denomination/price effects, while new FDI, people flows, tourism and seafood trade have turned cold. Most analysts characterise the period as a move from 'seirei keinetsu' (cold politics, hot economics) toward 'seirei keirei' (cold politics, cold economics) in investment and people-to-people dimensions, but not (yet) in aggregate goods trade.

**key_actors_positions**（主要行為者（日本政府、中國政府、其他）立場）

Short-term: Takaichi's Taiwan contingency remarks (Nov 2025) and Chinese retaliation; China's travel advisory and seafood ban; dual-use export bans and entity listings; yen weakness and price effects inflating yen-denominated trade values; AI/semiconductor equipment demand cycle; Middle East/Hormuz disruptions shifting Japan's overall trade balance.

Economic Coercion, Rare Earths and Export Controls (Japan-China, 2025-2027)

**current_status**（截至2026年10月的現狀概述）

> As of October 2026 China maintains a layered set of coercive economic measures against Japan, triggered by PM Takaichi's November 2025 Taiwan contingency remarks. (1) Seafood: China re-suspended imports of Japanese seafood on 19 November 2025, weeks after the first shipment (Hokkaido scallops, 5 Nov 2025) under the June 2025 partial reopening; the ban remains in place. (2) Dual-use export ban: MOFCOM Announcement No.1 of 2026 (6 Jan 2026) prohibits exports of all dual-use items to Japanese military end users/end uses or any use enhancing Japan's military capability, including re-exports by third countries of Chinese-origin items; it covers rare earths (seven medium/heavy elements), gallium, germanium, graphite, magnets and related technology. (3) Entity listings: on 24 Feb 2026 (Announcements No.11/12) 20 Japanese entities were put on the export control list (incl. MHI and KHI aerospace units, Mitsubishi Shipbuilding, National Defense Academy) and 20 on a watch list (incl. Subaru, ENEOS, Mitsubishi Materials); on 29 June 2026 China expanded the ban to 20 more entities (incl. National Institute for Defense Studies, MHI group firms) with watch-list additions (Mitsui E&S, Terra Drone, OKI group), citing Japanese 'remilitarisation'; Japan protested. (4) Rare earth flows: Chinese exports of controlled rare earths to Japan fell 51% y/y in H1 2026 (-88% in March, -82% in April, -81% in June); dysprosium/terbium exports to Japan were zero in H1 2026, with zero gallium, dysprosium, terbium and yttrium in June; per Kyodo (6 Jul 2026) no unalloyed Dy/Tb/Y/Sc shipped for six straight months. Argus reports Japanese manufacturers can secure only about two-thirds of heavy rare earth needs, with shortages expected to last until at least 2027. Japan's response focuses on diversification - Lynas/JARE offtake (5,000 t/yr NdPr, Lynas heavy rare earth share up to 75% to Japan through 2038), Sojitz imports of Australian heavy rare earths, JOGMEC investment in Namibia's Lofdal project (July 2026), India/Vietnam sourcing, Minamitorishima deep-sea mud test (50 t recovered Feb 2026), Japan-France recycling plant (2027) - plus G7/US coordination. On semiconductors, Japan maintains controls on 23+ categories of chipmaking equipment aligned with the US (since 2023, expanded April 2025) and faces US pressure (MATCH Act proposal, servicing restrictions) to go further, while Japanese equipment makers' China sales fall.

#### 分析

**key_actors_positions**（主要行為者（日本政府、中國政府、其他）立場）

> China (MOFCOM, MFA): frames measures as lawful national-security export controls against Japanese 'remilitarisation' and interference in Taiwan; insists civilian trade is unaffected but implements broad de facto slowdowns; links relaxation to Japan 'correcting' its Taiwan stance. Japan (Takaichi cabinet, METI, MOFA): protests each measure as unjustified economic coercion, demands withdrawal, refuses to retract Taiwan remarks; accelerates diversification (JOGMEC, JBIC, stockpiling, Economic Security Promotion Act) and coordinates with G7/US. Japanese industry (automakers, magnet makers, defence contractors, Keidanren): urges dialogue; seeks inventory, substitution (Dy-free magnets) and non-China supply; defence firms most directly affected. United States: formally urged China to restore rare earth exports to Japan; expands US-Japan critical minerals framework; but presses Japan to tighten chip-equipment controls. Australia (Lynas), India, Vietnam, France: alternative suppliers/partners.

**drivers**（推動關係走向的結構性與短期驅動因素）

> Structural: China's dominance in rare earth separation and magnets (about 80-90% globally; 63% of Japan's rare earth metal imports in 2024); China's codified export control regime (Export Control Law 2020, dual-use list) enabling targeted coercion; US-led technology controls and Chinese tit-for-tat logic; Japan's defence buildup and closer Taiwan alignment. Short-term: Takaichi's Taiwan remarks and refusal to retract; Japanese defence policy milestones; US-China truce leaving China's general rare earth controls paused but Japan-specific measures intact, making Japan a lower-cost target; China's desire to deter allies from Taiwan involvement (CSIS: first time mineral controls explicitly tied to Taiwan).

**risk_factors**（主要風險因素）

> Extension of curbs from military end users to broad civilian sectors (autos, electronics) via licence delays; expiry of the US-China pause (~Nov 2026) re-activating October 2025 extraterritorial rare earth rules; Japanese production stoppages from magnet shortages; retaliation spiral if Japan tightens chip equipment controls or servicing bans; seafood ban becoming long-term; secondary sanctions risk for third-country firms re-exporting Chinese items to listed Japanese entities; price spikes for Dy/Tb outside China; slow ramp of alternative sources (1-2 years for JOGMEC-backed projects, Minamitorishima commercial output uncertain).

Japanese public broadly supports diversification and does not favour concessions on Taiwan; Chinese public opinion endorses tough measures. ### US China Japan Triangle

#### 基本資訊

**item_name**（研究項目名稱）

> US-China-Japan Triangle: Trump's May 2026 Beijing visit, 'Japan-passing' risk in US-China deals, Xi's US state visit, G20 Miami, alliance burden-sharing and tariffs

#### 分析

**key_actors_positions**（主要行為者（日本政府、中國政府、其他）立場）

> United States (Trump): puts a transactional US-China truce first (soybeans, Boeing, fentanyl, rare earths, AI channel) and avoids taking sides publicly in the Japan-China dispute. He reassures Tokyo with praise and debrief calls but lets Xi's anti-Japan WWII framing into joint readouts. He reportedly urged Xi to engage Takaichi and to ease rare-earth curbs on Japan. He presses Japan for more defense spending (reportedly 3.5% of GDP), more host-nation support and delivery on the USD 550 billion investment. Taiwan arms sales are treated as a bargaining chip. The State Department and the ambassador in Tokyo (Glass) take a firmer pro-Japan line, calling China's measures coercion. China (Xi): uses its improved ties with Washington to isolate Japan and drive a wedge in the alliance. It invokes WWII 'militarism', insists China-Japan and China-US tracks be kept separate, refuses a leaders' meeting without a retraction, and has offered Premier Li Qiang at APEC. Japan (Takaichi): holds to her Taiwan statement while saying Japan's position on Taiwan is unchanged. She prioritizes alliance solidarity ('most mutually trusting of allies'), times diplomacy so she meets Trump before Xi does, uses investment delivery to fend off burden-sharing demands, and seeks a Xi meeting at APEC Shenzhen. Others: Taiwan worries about being traded away and has passed a USD 30 billion special defense budget. South Korea has gained as the alternative destination for Chinese demand.

**drivers**（推動關係走向的結構性與短期驅動因素）

> Structural: (1) US-China great-power bargaining under Trump's transactional, tariff-centred approach, which makes allies' interests negotiable. (2) The Taiwan question links Japan's security to US-China understandings. (3) China's leverage over rare earths and critical minerals, which the US itself depends on through Japanese suppliers. (4) Japan's dependence on the US alliance and US market, which gives Washington leverage on cost-sharing and investment. (5) Historical memory (80th anniversary of WWII in 2025, 55th anniversary of Japan-China normalization in 2027) used as narrative ammunition. Short-term: (1) A packed summit calendar (Beijing May, Washington Sep, APEC Shenzhen Nov, G20 Miami Dec, Trump-Xi visits planned for 2027) gives Beijing incentives to keep Washington sweet while squeezing Tokyo. (2) Trump's need for farm and Boeing deals before and after the November 2026 US midterms. (3) Legal turmoil over US tariffs after the Supreme Court IEEPA ruling and the Section 122 and Section 301 replacements. (4) Takaichi's domestic strength after the February 2026 election landslide, which reduces pressure on her to retract. (5) Host-nation-support talks ahead of the March 2027 SMA expiry.

**risk_factors**（主要風險因素）

> 1) 'Japan passing': a Trump-Xi package (for example on Taiwan language such as 'opposing' independence, arms-sale restraint, or tolerating China's pressure on Japan) concluded without Japan's input, which would erode deterrence credibility. 2) US tacit acceptance of China's WWII narrative targeting Japan. 3) Collapse of the fragile US-China truce over chips or rare earths, which would put Japan on the front line of decoupling and secondary controls. 4) Escalating US burden-sharing demands (3.5% of GDP, sharply higher host-nation support) clashing with Japan's fiscal limits, plus tariff stacking (Section 301 or 232 actions, the excess-capacity probe). 5) Uncertainty over the US investment framework's legal basis after the IEEPA ruling, and Japanese domestic criticism of the 'tribute' optics of the USD 550 billion pledge. 6) Military incidents around the Senkakus or Taiwan (such as the Dec 2025 radar lock-on) testing Trump's willingness to back Japan publicly. 7) China using Trump's 2027 hosting and visit diplomacy to extract further concessions on Taiwan.

**narrative_and_public_opinion**（雙方官方敘事與民意）

> China frames Japan as reviving 'militarism' or 'neo-militarism' and casts China and the US as WWII allies against Japanese aggression, a framing it inserted into US-China summit readouts. Japan stresses that its Taiwan stance is unchanged and that the US-Japan alliance is 'unshakable' and 'the most mutually trusting', presenting Trump's debriefs as proof Tokyo is not being bypassed. Japanese media (SCMP, Nikkei and others) debated the contrast between Xi's red-carpet reception and Takaichi's lower-key US visits. Japanese public opinion backs Takaichi (cabinet approval about 70-75% in late 2025; LDP supermajority in Feb 2026), but a majority (57%, Asahi, Sep 2026) now wants her to work on improving China ties.

#### 展望

**outlook_2027**（2027年展望）

> 2027 is likely to bring deeper US-China institutionalization (Board of Trade and Board of Investment, AI dialogue) and possibly more reciprocal leader visits, alongside persistent risk of the truce breaking over technology controls. Japan faces three pressure points: (1) concluding the new host-nation-support SMA before the March 2027 expiry, probably with a modest increase tied to Japan's defense build-up, and continued pressure for spending above 2% of GDP toward 3-3.5%; (2) tariff and investment frictions under Section 301 and 232 and project selection under the USD 550 billion fund; (3) the risk that US-China understandings on Taiwan narrow Japan's room. The 55th anniversary of Japan-China normalization (September 29, 2027) offers a natural off-ramp for a Japan-China thaw, which Washington would welcome. A gradual Japan-China easing in 2027 (foreign ministers' meeting, then leaders' meeting) is plausible if Tokyo finds a face-saving formula. Japan will hedge by deepening ties with Australia, the Philippines, South Korea and Europe in case of further 'Japan passing'. Uncertainty is high given Trump's unpredictability, the 2028 US election cycle starting, and possible PLA activity around Taiwan.

**key_actors_positions**（主要行為者（日本政府、中國政府、其他）立場）

Japanese business and local governments: tourism-dependent areas (Osaka, Kyoto, Hokkaido, Kyushu, department stores, ferries) are hit and lobby for dialogue. Lawmakers: cross-party and LDP pro-China figures (Iwaya, Komura, Gaku Hashimoto) act as back-channels. Others: Hong Kong and Macau authorities relayed the advisory, and event organizers in HK cancelled Japanese acts. South Korea and Southeast Asia gain diverted Chinese tourists.

**drivers**（推動關係走向的結構性與短期驅動因素）

> Structural: (1) China's state ability to steer outbound tourism through advisories, airline capacity, group-tour quotas and travel-agency guidance, a tool used before against South Korea (2017 THAAD), Japan (2012) and others. (2) Deep negative mutual perceptions (about 90% negative in both countries in the 2024 Genron poll) and historical issues. (3) Japan's reliance on Chinese visitors (about 23% of arrivals and the largest share of spending in 2024-25). (4) Japan's large Chinese student population (over 120,000, about 41% of international higher-education students). Short-term: (1) Takaichi's refusal to retract and Beijing's linkage of normalization to a retraction. (2) Security incidents involving Chinese nationals in Japan, used to justify advisories. (3) The US-China détente reducing China's need to court Japan. (4) Japanese domestic politics (LDP supermajority, support for a firm stance) versus economic lobbying. (5) The APEC Shenzhen and 2027 55th-anniversary calendar as possible occasions for a thaw.

**risk_factors**（主要風險因素）

> 1) The advisories becoming semi-permanent, structurally shifting Chinese outbound demand to South Korea, Southeast Asia and Europe, so tourism does not recover quickly even after a political thaw. 2) Incidents involving Chinese nationals in Japan (crime, hate incidents, detentions), or Japanese nationals in China (espionage detentions, attacks like the 2024 Shenzhen school stabbing), triggering new advisories or nationalist outbursts. 3) Further cultural bans, such as wider bans on Japanese anime, games or celebrities, and online nationalist campaigns. 4) A decline in Chinese students choosing Japan, hurting Japanese universities and language schools, and suspension of academic exchanges. 5) Loss of the Genron poll and Beijing-Tokyo Forum as dialogue platforms. 6) Persisting Japanese public antipathy limiting the government's room to compromise. 7) Airline route rights and slots lost to competitors (South Korean and Southeast Asian carriers), slowing the eventual recovery of capacity.

**current_status**（截至2026年10月的現狀概述）

> As of late October 2026, the safety of people moving between the two countries has become a central part of the Japan-China crisis that began with PM Sanae Takaichi's Diet remarks on Taiwan on 7 November 2025. (1) Japanese in China: Two Fuji Electric employees were detained in Dalian on 18 and 25 May 2026 on suspicion of smuggling banned goods. Reports tie the case to China's rare-earth export controls. Both were formally arrested in mid- and late June 2026. Japanese officials say the case does not appear to involve espionage, but China has released no details. The Astellas Pharma employee detained in March 2023 was sentenced in Beijing in July 2025 to 3.5 years for espionage. He did not appeal and is serving the sentence. MOFA counts 17 Japanese detained on 'national security' charges since China's 2014 counter-espionage law (revised and broadened in July 2023), with five still held as of mid-2025. The Fuji Electric pair are an additional, non-espionage export-control case. On 19 May 2026 a man attacked a Japanese restaurant in the Shanghai World Financial Center with a knife, injuring two Japanese (Mori Building employees) and one Chinese national. Beijing called it an 'isolated' case involving a mentally ill attacker, and Tokyo asked Beijing to guarantee its nationals' safety. These events follow the 2024 Suzhou and Shenzhen attacks. The Japanese resident community in China keeps shrinking: 97,538 in October 2024, down from more than 150,000 in 2012, and Japanese-school enrollment is falling. Japanese tourist travel to China fell roughly 90% in early 2026. (2) Chinese in Japan: Chinese nationals are the largest foreign group, at 930,428 at end-2025 (22.6% of 4.125 million foreign residents). The total foreign population reached a record 4,281,026 by end-June 2026, and the Chinese figure kept growing despite poor ties. Since 14 November 2025 Beijing has kept a travel advisory against Japan and repeatedly reissued safety warnings. These cited a 31 December 2025 Shinjuku vehicle incident, Tokyo robberies (January 2026), 'hit-and-run gangs' (March 2026), and the 24 March 2026 intrusion into the Chinese Embassy by a knife-carrying Ground Self-Defense Force member, who was arrested and later dismissed. A separate online bomb threat followed. Chinese visitor arrivals have run 45-61% below the prior year every month. Tokyo has tightened its foreign-resident regime. It adopted 'Comprehensive Measures for Accepting Foreign Nationals and Orderly Coexistence' in January 2026. Nationality reporting is now required for land and building registration from FY2026. Naturalization residency requirements are being extended toward 10 years. On 1 October 2026 the permanent-residency guidelines were revised: stricter income, pension and Japanese-language conditions, mostly for applications from April 2027, and the fee rises from 10,000 yen to 200,000 yen. On counter-intelligence, the Diet enacted a law in May 2026 creating a National Intelligence Council. An expert panel began on 28 September 2026 and started work on a foreign-agent registration system on 26 October 2026, toward an anti-espionage / foreign-interference law, probably for the 2027 ordinary Diet session. Beijing frames these measures as discriminatory and 'neo-militarist', and Tokyo frames them as 'orderly coexistence' and closing Japan's status as a 'spy paradise'.

#### 分析

**key_actors_positions**（主要行為者（日本政府、中國政府、其他）立場）

- **Japan_government**: Demands early release of detained nationals (Fuji Electric pair, Astellas employee and others), transparency on charges, and consular access, which it has obtained. It presses Beijing to ensure Japanese safety after each violent incident. Domestically it pursues 'orderly coexistence': tighter residency, naturalization and land rules, a National Intelligence Council, and a planned anti-espionage / foreign-agent registration law. It insists none of this targets a specific nationality. It calls the embassy intrusion 'regrettable' and prosecuted the intruder.
- **China_government**: Treats detentions as routine law enforcement ('China is a country of rule of law') under its counter-espionage law and export-control regime. It calls attacks on Japanese 'isolated incidents'. It uses travel and study advisories and repeated safety warnings about Japan as both consular protection and economic pressure. It frames Japan's foreign-resident tightening and anti-spy push, together with the embassy intrusion, as evidence of rising xenophobia and 'neo-militarism'. It links normalization of people-to-people exchange to Takaichi retracting her Taiwan remarks.
- **Japanese_business**: Keidanren, the Japan-China Economic Association and the Japanese Chamber of Commerce in China worry about personnel safety and the unpredictability of export-control enforcement, and Fuji Electric declined comment. Many firms are cutting expatriate postings, localizing management and restricting business travel. The Japan-China Economic Association skipped its January 2026 Beijing mission for the first time since 2012 apart from 2020.
- **Chinese_community_in_Japan**: Continues to grow (students, highly skilled professionals, business managers and family members). It reports anxiety over online hate speech and the tightened PR and naturalization rules, but has not shown significant net outflow. Chinese investors face new nationality reporting for property.
- **Japanese_right_and_civil_society**: Conservative LDP members and Sanseito-style populists push tougher foreigner and land rules. Fifteen rights groups and UN rapporteur standards warn that a broad foreign-agent law could chill civil society and press freedom.

**drivers**（推動關係走向的結構性與短期驅動因素）

### History Issues and 2027 Anniversaries

*歷史問題與敏感紀念日：靖國參拜、2027年7月7日盧溝橋事變90週年、9月29日邦交正常化55週年、四個政治文件的解釋爭議*

#### 基本資訊

**item_name**（研究項目名稱）

> History issues and sensitive anniversaries (Yasukuni; 90th anniversary of the Marco Polo Bridge Incident on 7 July 2027; 55th anniversary of normalization on 29 September 2027; disputes over interpreting the four political documents)

**current_status**（截至2026年10月的現狀概述）

> As of late October 2026, history issues have merged with the Taiwan dispute that began with PM Sanae Takaichi's Diet remarks of 7 November 2025. Beijing argues that the remarks violate the 'four political documents': the 1972 Joint Communiqué, the 1978 Treaty of Peace and Friendship, the 1998 Joint Declaration and the 2008 Joint Statement. It also frames them as a revival of Japanese militarism, linking Taiwan to Japan's 1895-1945 colonial rule. In its UN letters (22 Nov and 1 Dec 2025) and embassy statements it invoked the Cairo and Potsdam Declarations, the 1931 Mukden Incident and the UN Charter 'enemy state' clauses. Chinese state media also revived the 'undetermined status of Ryukyu' theme. Tokyo's position is that its Taiwan stance is unchanged and rests on the 1972 Communiqué, in which Japan 'fully understands and respects' the PRC position and 'firmly maintains its stand under Article 8 of the Potsdam Declaration'. Tokyo has not retracted the remarks. On 7 October 2026 former FM Takeshi Iwaya disclosed that Wang Yi had rejected his proposal that both governments reaffirm the 1972 Communiqué as a de facto withdrawal of the remarks. The interpretation of the four documents is therefore the core diplomatic obstacle. On Yasukuni, Takaichi has avoided in-person visits since becoming LDP president, although she had visited regularly before. She sent masakaki offerings for the spring festival (21 April 2026) and the autumn festival (17 October 2026), and a personal offering on 15 August 2026. Four cabinet ministers, including Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi, visited on 15 August 2026, the most in six years. China 'strongly condemned' this and lodged protests, and South Korea expressed deep regret. China held its usual commemorations: Nanjing Memorial Day on 13 December 2025, with Shi Taifeng warning that any revival of militarism will fail, and the 89th Marco Polo Bridge anniversary on 7 July 2026, with a PLA Rocket Force missile poster. The approaching cluster of anniversaries in 2027 is unusually dense. It includes the 90th anniversary of the start of the full-scale war (7 July), the battle of Shanghai (13 August), the 55th anniversary of normalization (29 September), and the 90th anniversary of the Nanjing Massacre (13 December). Political calendars coincide: the LDP presidential race around September 2027 and China's 21st Party Congress in autumn 2027. Together these make 2027 a structurally high-risk year for history-driven friction, but also a possible window for a symbolic reset if a leaders' meeting occurs.

#### 分析

**key_actors_positions**（主要行為者（日本政府、中國政府、其他）立場）

- **Japan_government**: Maintains that its Taiwan position under the 1972 Communiqué is unchanged and that the four documents remain the foundation of relations. It will not retract the 7 Nov remarks but avoids repeating specifics. It upholds past cabinets' positions on history 'as a whole', including the Murayama and Abe 70th-anniversary statements, without issuing new apologies. The PM restrains herself from visiting Yasukuni in person while making offerings, and lets ministers visit in a 'private capacity'. It rejects 'enemy state' clause and Ryukyu arguments as obsolete or baseless.
- **China_government**: Holds that the four documents, especially the 1972 Communiqué on Taiwan and history, are binding and that Takaichi breached them. It demands retraction as the condition for normalization. It uses anniversaries (7 Jul, 15 Aug, 3 Sep, 18 Sep, 25 Oct, 13 Dec) for anti-militarism messaging. It links Yasukuni and Taiwan into a single narrative of 'reviving militarism', and makes selective use of the UN Charter, Cairo/Potsdam and Ryukyu arguments to raise pressure.
- **South_Korea**: Criticizes each Yasukuni offering and visit ('deep disappointment and regret') but keeps Japan ties stable under Lee Jae-myung. It does not join China's Taiwan framing.
- **United_States**: Generally avoids comment on Yasukuni under Trump. It supports Japan's Taiwan-related defense posture but wants to avoid escalation, and US-China summitry may affect Beijing's incentives.
- **Taiwan**: Welcomes Takaichi's stance. China ties the 'Taiwan Recovery' and 1945 narrative directly to Japan's wartime history.
- **Japanese_domestic_actors**: The conservative LDP base and Nippon Kaigi favor Yasukuni visits. Coalition partner Ishin is more hawkish than the former Komeito partner. Opposition (CDP) figures urged Takaichi to withdraw the remarks. Former PMs and pro-China lawmakers (Iwaya, Hashimoto, Komura delegations) seek a face-saving formula.

**drivers**（推動關係走向的結構性與短期驅動因素）

- **structural**: The CCP's legitimacy narrative built on victory in the War of Resistance and 'national humiliation' memory, intensified after the 2025 80th anniversary., Japanese conservative identity politics around Yasukuni and the 'apology fatigue' generation, and Takaichi's own record as a regular Yasukuni visitor., Deliberate ambiguity in the 1972 Communiqué ('understands and respects' rather than 'recognizes') that permits rival readings on Taiwan., Fusion of the Taiwan question with history: Taiwan as a former Japanese colony, Cairo/Potsdam as the legal basis for China's claim., A dense cycle of decade anniversaries (80th in 2025; 90th of 1937 events in 2027; 55th of normalization).
- **short_term**: The unresolved Takaichi remarks and China's insistence on retraction., Leadership calendars: Takaichi's LDP presidential term expires around September 2027, and China's 21st Party Congress is in autumn 2027. Both reduce leaders' room for concessions., Cabinet ministers' Yasukuni visits (e.g., Koizumi on 15 Aug 2026) as signals to the domestic base., Chinese use of history to justify economic coercion (export controls, travel advisories) and to rally international audiences (UN letters).

**risk_factors**（主要風險因素）

- In-person Yasukuni visit by Takaichi (spring or autumn festival, or 15 August), especially in 2027 if her domestic position weakens or before the LDP presidential race. This would likely trigger severe Chinese measures.
- 90th anniversaries in 2027 (7 Jul Marco Polo Bridge, 13 Aug Shanghai, 13 Dec Nanjing) bringing high-level Chinese commemorations, possibly with Xi speeches explicitly linking Japan's Taiwan policy to militarism, and nationalist mobilization endangering Japanese residents.
- China formalizing the 'undetermined Ryukyu status' or 'enemy state' arguments in official documents, which would escalate history into a sovereignty dispute.
- Failure to agree on any joint observance of the 55th normalization anniversary, deepening the perception that the four-document framework has eroded.
- Japanese textbook screening results (spring 2027) or statements by officials on Nanjing or comfort women sparking protests.
- Japanese defense moves (counter-strike deployments, constitutional revision debate, SDF Taiwan-contingency exercises) framed by China as militarism around anniversaries.

> As of late October 2026, the China-Japan contest has spread from diplomacy into international law and the information space, and both sides have made their positions harder to walk back. (1) UN letters: China's UN Permanent Representative Fu Cong wrote twice to Secretary-General Guterres, on 21 Nov 2025 and 1 Dec 2025, and asked that both letters be circulated as General Assembly documents. The letters called PM Takaichi's 7 Nov 2025 Diet remark that a Taiwan contingency could be a 'survival-threatening situation' a threat to the postwar order, demanded a retraction, and warned that any Japanese armed intervention in the Taiwan Strait would be an act of aggression against which China would exercise self-defence under the UN Charter. Japan's PR Kazuyuki Yamazaki replied on 24 Nov 2025 that Japan's position is unchanged. (2) Enemy-state clauses: on 21 Nov 2025 the Chinese Embassy in Japan cited Articles 53 and 107 of the UN Charter on X, implying that founding members could act against Japan without Security Council authorisation. Tokyo called the clauses obsolete, citing the 1995 UNGA resolution (which China supported) and the 2005 World Summit Outcome. In August 2026 Russian FM Lavrov told Japan to read Article 107, and the Chinese MFA (Lin Jian, 19 Aug) publicly agreed. At UNGA-81 on 22-23 Sep 2026 Takaichi called for the clauses to be deleted. On 28 Sep, in a right of reply, China's deputy PR Sun Lei said they must not be altered given Japan's 're-militarisation', and North Korea called Japan's request 'shameful'. Japan's deputy PR Shigeru Umetsu rebutted. Deletion requires a Charter amendment that all P5 members must ratify, so China and Russia can block it indefinitely. (3) Ryukyu: since Nov 2025 Chinese state media (CGTN, PLA Daily) and CASS-affiliated scholars have promoted the claim that the status of Ryukyu is 'undetermined'. Kyodo counted a roughly 20-fold increase in Ryukyu/Okinawa-related Chinese reports. The PRC MFA has not formally adopted the position, and this is the third time this century Beijing has played the 'Ryukyu card'. (4) Militarism and nuclear narratives: Beijing systematically labels Japan as 'neo-militarist', citing the security-document revision, the scrapping of arms-export limits (Apr 2026), Japan's first overseas offensive missile test (May 2026) and nuclear-powered submarine talk. On 8 Jan 2026 CACDA and CINIS jointly published a report on Japan's 'nuclear ambitions', citing 44.4 t of separated plutonium. On 30 Apr 2026 the PRC MFA issued an NPT working paper warning of a Japanese 'nuclear breakout', and Chinese diplomats repeated the charge at the 2026 NPT Review Conference (29 Apr and 4 May). The Defense Ministry (June) and a PLA Rocket Force-related report (September) continued the line. Japan's own ambiguity on the third non-nuclear principle, 'not introducing', supplies material for this narrative, but the claim that Japan is pursuing nuclear weapons is unsubstantiated. (5) Japan's counter-measures: the Defense Ministry and SDF opened official X accounts for rapid rebuttal, and the year-end revision of the three security documents is expected to name 'cognitive warfare' countermeasures and strategic communications. The 2026 Defense White Paper calls China Japan's 'greatest strategic challenge'. The narrative contest is ongoing and shows no sign of de-escalating. China's legal framing (Cairo/Potsdam, the 'Taiwan Recovery Day' set on 25 Oct 2025 and marked again on 25 Oct 2026, the enemy-state clauses) is being built into a lasting body of argument to challenge Japan's standing to act on Taiwan.

#### 分析

**key_actors_positions**（主要行為者（日本政府、中國政府、其他）立場）

> Takaichi and the LDP mainstream: accelerate the defence build-up (counterstrike, long-range missiles, drones and AI), revise the documents a year early, name China the greatest strategic challenge, ease arms exports, build an intelligence community and anti-espionage law, and keep the non-nuclear principles 'as policy' while debating the third one. Ishin (coalition partner): more hawkish on some items, including early nuclear-powered submarines and a review of the third non-nuclear principle; pushes Diet seat reduction and constitutional amendment. Komeito (now in opposition): defends the non-nuclear principles and caution toward China. Public: supports a stronger defence but about 75% want the non-nuclear principles kept, and cabinet approval is falling. United States (Trump administration): presses for 3.5% of GDP (5% including related spending) and welcomes the build-up. **drivers**（推動關係走向的結構性與短期驅動因素）

> Structural: China's military growth and grey-zone pressure around the Senkakus and the Southwest Islands; the Taiwan contingency debate; China-Russia joint bomber flights and exercises; North Korean missiles; US burden-sharing demands and doubts over extended deterrence under Trump; generational change in the LDP toward a security-hawk consensus; economic security exposure to Chinese rare-earth and dual-use export controls. **policy_tools_and_reversibility**（雙方使用的政策工具/措施、啟動日、可逆性與解除條件）

> 1) Revision of the three security documents (Cabinet approval due December 2026): a 5-to-10-year framework that is hard to reverse once adopted. Its rare-earth and critical-mineral diversification and overseas loss-absorption schemes are structural de-risking from China. 7) Counterstrike capability (Tomahawk and Type-12 deployments from FY2025-26): a hardware commitment that is irreversible in the medium term. Conditions for softening: improved China ties (for example after an APEC summit) or coalition and opposition pressure. US demands push the other way.

#### 分析

**key_actors_positions**（主要行為者（日本政府、中國政府、其他）立場）

- **Xi Jinping / CPC Central leadership**: Treats Takaichi's remark as a breach of the 'political foundation' (1972 Communique, Taiwan clause) and a test of resolve; requires retraction. Wants a stable external environment for the 2027 congress but cannot appear weak on Taiwan or Japan before it.
- **MFA / Wang Yi**: Public hard line ('reflect, correct, retract'); uses meetings with pro-dialogue Japanese politicians (Iwaya, Diet delegations) to signal conditions while bypassing the Takaichi cabinet.
- **MOFCOM**: Implements calibrated economic coercion (dual-use ban, entity watchlist) framed as 'military end-use only', preserving deniability and reversibility.
- **PLA / CMC**: Under deep purge; leadership prioritizes loyalty and 2027 centenary readiness; maintains pressure via carrier ops, Senkaku patrols, joint patrols with Russia.
- **CCDI and propaganda apparatus**: Emphasize discipline and anti-'militarism' narrative; state media (Global Times, CGTN) frames Takaichi as reviving militarism.
- **Japanese government (Takaichi)**: Refuses retraction; says position consistent with 1972 Communique; seeks 'mutually beneficial strategic relationship' and open dialogue; strengthened by Feb 2026 supermajority.

**drivers**（推動關係走向的結構性與短期驅動因素）

- **short_term**: Stabilized US-China relations reduce need to court Tokyo and allow Beijing to isolate Japan from Washington ('wedge')., Takaichi's electoral strength removes expectation she will fall soon, forcing Beijing to choose between prolonged freeze or face-saving thaw., APEC Shenzhen (Nov 2026) host role: incentive to appear magnanimous and avoid a boycott narrative., Military purge turbulence: leadership may avoid risky military escalation while keeping gray-zone pressure.

**risk_factors**（主要風險因素）

- Pre-Congress rigidity: no leader can be seen softening on Taiwan before autumn 2027; risk the freeze lasts through 2027.

- Heavy rare earth curbs could be widened from 'military end-use' to broad civilian denial, hitting Japanese autos/electronics.
- Historical anniversaries in 2027 (90th of Marco Polo Bridge 7 Jul; 90th of Nanjing 13 Dec) amplify nationalist mobilization.
- Detentions of Japanese nationals, attacks on Japanese citizens, or boycott campaigns.
- Taiwan politics (Nov 2026 local elections, 2028 presidential campaign) and US arms sales could trigger PLA drills that involve Japanese territory/EEZ.
- Economic stress could push Beijing to both court and coerce, raising policy volatility.

**third_party_variables**（美國、台灣、韓國、俄羅斯、朝鮮、東協、G7等第三方變數的影響方向）

- **United States**: US-China detente (May and Sept 2026 summits, truce extension) reduces US appetite to back Tokyo loudly; Beijing exploits wedge. A US-China rupture would harden Beijing but also raise its incentive to peel Japan away.
